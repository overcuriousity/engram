{# The one line under the box, and the only guidance an idle page carries. It replaces two: a sentence claiming that typing searches as you go, and a second copy of the accepted file types that the Attach button's own `title` and the input's `aria-label` already carry. An example teaches the first better than the claim did, and the second was never guidance — the picker a phone opens names what it will accept itself. The examples are the classifier's own prototypes, in the reader's language: see `moments::examples_for`. Nothing here is a second copy of anything, so the page cannot come to teach a phrasing the base has stopped recognising. A chip fills the box and does not submit. Pressing one puts the phrasing in front of you and fires the echo below it, which is the whole loop in one press — and a press is still yours to make. The chips are here in every state, and the idle column's list of what a paste becomes carries the two phrasings as glosses rather than as a second pair of buttons. They used to move: while the base was young the buttons stood in that list and this line went without them. Two problems with that, and they are the same problem. This paragraph is what the textarea names in `aria-describedby`, so a reader who is told about the box was told about nothing pressable; and this paragraph is the only one of the two that survives the first keystroke, so the chips were unreachable exactly when somebody is typing. Standing still, they also stop needing a `teach` flag threaded through two templates to keep them from appearing twice. With nothing held the opening clause says whose base this is, which is what a person wants to know before pasting their own notes onto a server. #}
